Scene-by-scene version

Write-In

A school election, a former best friend, and a duck who never left.

Panel 1 · The only voter who listens

Every morning at 7:40, Dara Kim practiced her campaign speech for the only voter who never interrupted her.

"Fellow students," she told the pond, "this school deserves a president who actually listens."

Captain Waddles, the school duck, tilted his head. He had arrived two years ago, uninvited, and simply never left. Nobody knew who had named him Captain. Nobody had dared to demote him.

When Dara talked to him, the speech came out smooth as glass. No notes. No shaking. Then she checked her blue index cards anyway — twelve of them, rubber-banded, color-coded — because a duck is not a gym full of eight hundred people.

Captain Waddles watched the cards the way he usually watched bread.

Panel 2 · Two candidates

The election was on Friday. There were two candidates: Dara Kim, who had written a nine-point plan, and Leo Han, who had written his name.

That was Leo's whole poster. His name, in marker. Around it, little orange duck footprints had started to appear — stickers some sixth grader had printed, which were now turning up on lockers, lunch trays and one very confused math teacher.

At the end of the hall, Leo was laughing with the soccer team. Two years ago he had laughed like that at Dara's lunch table, at Dara's terrible jokes. Then seventh grade happened, and soccer, and new people, and somehow neither of them had ever said anything about it.

Dara pressed the tape onto her poster much harder than tape needs to be pressed.

Panel 3 · Half a sentence

On Thursday, she passed Room 114. The door was open just a crack.

"…Dara only runs for things," Leo was saying, "so people will clap for her."

Somebody laughed. The door swung shut.

Dara stood very still, holding her twelve cards against her chest. Her face felt hot and her hands felt cold, which shouldn't have been physically possible, and yet.

So that was what he thought. Fine. She would win, and they could all clap.

Panel 4 · Friday, 8:05

On Friday morning she went to the pond to run through the speech one last time. But her voice wouldn't come out smooth. It kept snagging on half a sentence.

She was so busy being angry that she didn't notice Captain Waddles stepping closer.

Snap. The rubber band went. Twelve blue cards burst into the air like startled birds and came down on the pond, one by one, the ink blooming into the water.

The assembly started in ten minutes.

Panel 5 · Empty hands

Eight hundred students packed the bleachers. A row of sixth graders had duck footprints stuck on their cheeks and were quacking softly, in unison, which the principal, Ms. Park, had decided to ignore.

Dara sat on the stage with nothing in her hands. Next to her, Leo didn't have any cards either. He never did.

"Don't worry," he whispered. "Nobody really listens to these things."

She didn't answer. She was busy looking at her empty palms.

Panel 6 · The speech

At the podium, Dara opened her mouth, and nothing came out.

Then, at the back of the gym, the double door creaked. Captain Waddles walked in, slow and important, as if he had a seat reserved.

So she talked to him.

She skipped the nine-point plan. She said she missed when lunch was loud for the right reasons. She said that some people in this school felt like the background of somebody else's story, and that she had been one of them for a while. She said she didn't want anyone to clap. She just wanted people to feel noticed.

Nobody clapped right away. Then everybody did.

Panel 7 · The other half

Leo didn't walk to the podium. He just leaned over and pulled the microphone down.

"Yesterday I said something," he said, "and I think somebody only heard half of it." He turned to Dara. "What I said was: Dara only runs for things she actually believes in. I just run so people will clap for me."

He shrugged at eight hundred people. "So, honestly? Vote for her."

Dara stared at him. For the first time in two years, he stared back.

Panel 8 · The count

After the speeches, Ms. Park tipped the ballot box over onto the table on the stage.

Dara Kim: 241. Leo Han: 98.

Write-in: 463.

Every single write-in ballot had the same drawing on it. A duck.

Ms. Park checked the rule book. She checked it again. "'Any member of the school community,'" she read slowly, and closed it. "Well. He has been here longer than most of you."

Dara looked at Leo. Leo looked at Dara. And then they were laughing so hard that the math teacher with the duck sticker had to sit down.

Panel 9 · President Waddles

Captain Waddles was sworn in that afternoon, beside the pond, wearing a tiny red sash that somebody's grandmother had sewn overnight.

His first act as president was to build a nest out of every campaign poster in the building — and twelve soggy blue index cards.

His second act was to appoint two advisors. Mostly he did this by refusing to take bread from anyone else.

"For the record," Leo said, holding out a crust, "I voted for you."

Dara smoothed out one of her old cards. It now had a muddy footprint stamped right in the middle, like a signature.

"For the record," she said, "I voted for the duck."

Leo thought about this. "Fair."
